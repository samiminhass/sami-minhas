# Character Design Audit: Morrow, Vesper, and Nix

Audit of the stage 2 grayscale blockout against established character design
research. Each score is from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong), judged from the renders in
`blender/renders/stage2`. The goal is clarity, appeal, and screen presence in
vertical short videos, not a promise of views.

## The research, organized into ten lenses

| # | Lens | What the research says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Silhouette | A design should still read as a black shape or a tiny thumbnail. This is the first test. |
| 2 | Shape language | Each character needs one dominant shape. Circles read as soft and friendly, squares as stable, triangles as dynamic or dangerous. |
| 3 | Big, medium, small | A design needs all three shape sizes, each clearly different from the next. |
| 4 | Detail distribution | Keep about 70 percent of a form calm and put the detail in about 30 percent. This gives the eye a place to rest and a place to land. |
| 5 | Proportion and exaggeration | Proportions carry personality. Push them further than feels safe, then pull back. |
| 6 | Face and expression | Expression range must read at small sizes. Eyes and brows do most of the work. |
| 7 | Focal feature | Each character should have one feature the eye finds first. |
| 8 | Pose and line of action | A pose needs one clear line of energy. Symmetric poses look dull and lifeless. |
| 9 | Contrast with the world | Value and color separation from the background decides whether a character pops on a phone. |
| 10 | Originality | A design should not collide with a famous existing character. |

Lenses 1 to 8 come from the sources listed at the end. Lenses 9 and 10 are standard
production practice rather than findings from this search.

**Vesper is the weakest character, and the whole cast is weakest in lenses 7 and

yet makes the characters separate from a background.

## Where each character lags

### Morrow: 30 of 50

**What works.** The seed silhouette is soft and friendly. The forward lean reads in
side view. The face patch gives a clear dark frame for the eyes. The relationship
pose already shows curiosity and self-protection.

**Where it lags.**

- **The focal feature is lost at phone size.** The cavity and ember are the heart of
  the character, but the ember is a tiny bead. In the thumbnail test it disappears.
- **Face and cavity compete.** Both sit in the middle of the body at similar sizes,
  so the eye has two targets and settles on neither.
- **The face has no brows.** Worry, determination, and embarrassment are hard to
  show with eyelids alone. The mouth is too small to read on a phone.
- **The limbs are all one size.** Arms and legs are similar tubes, which breaks the
  big, medium, small rule. The hands become fork shapes at thumbnail size.
- **The rest pose is a product pose.** Arms hang straight and the stance is almost
  symmetric. The character's story, carrying forgotten dreams, is not visible.
- **Originality risk.** "Egg with legs and a face" is a crowded archetype. The
  cavity is what saves it, so the cavity has to be bigger.

### Vesper: 21 of 50

**What works.** The side view S-curve and hunched hood are distinctive. The
clasped hands sell "overly formal" instantly. The broken halo is a genuinely
unusual idea.

**Where it lags.**

- **It collides with No-Face from Spirited Away.** A tall, dark, robed body with a
  pale oval mask is the exact recipe of that famous character. This is the most
  serious problem in the cast. The brief says the face should be dark stone, but
  the blockout mask renders lighter than the body.
- **The front view reads as straight vertical planks.** Rectangular panels say
  "stable" and "boxy", not "mysterious". The crescent motif is missing from the
  biggest shapes on the character.
- **No calm areas.** Every panel is similar in size, so detail is spread evenly
  and the body looks busy without having a focus.
- **The halo and fragments are too thin.** The best idea on the character
  vanishes at thumbnail size.
- **The hands are not exaggerated.** The brief asks for long hands that can shelter
  Morrow. Right now they are ordinary-sized.
- **The face is almost blank.** The eyes are small, so the mask reads as a stare
  rather than as patient, anxious, or dryly funny.

### Nix: 29 of 50

**What works.** The crescent reads in one glance, even as a tiny silhouette. The
bite out of the horn makes it feel broken and specific. The edge-on view is a
built-in "trying to disappear" gag.

**Where it lags.**

- **It has no contrast yet.** Nix is the darkest object in the cast, and its coral
  edge is not built. On a dark scene it would vanish completely. This is the
  biggest single gap in the cast.
- **The eyes have no lids.** Two plain ovals can only look surprised. Mischief,
  smugness, and embarrassment all need lid shapes.
- **The bead is too small** to show where Nix's attention is pointing.
- **The fins are triangles.** They add sharp shapes that fight the crescent and make
  it busier than it needs to be.
- **Originality risk.** A crescent moon with a face is a common icon. The bite, the
  bead, and the fins are what make it Nix, so they need to be bolder.

## Cast-level gaps that limit screen presence

1. **The three characters cannot share a vertical frame.** Vesper is about ten
   times taller than Nix. In a 9:16 frame, either Vesper's head is cut off or Nix
   is a dot. The designs need built-in staging poses: Vesper bending into a deep
   crescent curve, Nix perching on Vesper's halo or Morrow's crest.
2. **No signature poses.** Each character needs one instantly recognizable resting
   pose and one "hero" pose to use in thumbnails and video openings.
3. **No sound identity.** Each character's signature feature could carry a sound,
   which makes them recognizable even off screen.
4. **The motifs are uneven.** The crescent shows clearly on Morrow and Nix but
   barely on Vesper's big shapes.

## Upgrade plan, ranked by impact

These become the brief for stage 3. Each one makes an existing idea bigger and
clearer. None adds accessories.

| Rank | Upgrade | Character | Lenses fixed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Make the face dark carved stone with a crescent-shaped profile, not a pale oval. Make the eyes the lightest part. | Vesper | 6, 9, 10 |
| 2 | Build the coral edge along the inner curve of the crescent, so Nix reads on any background. | Nix | 7, 9 |
| 3 | Enlarge the cavity by about 30 percent and the ember to about twice its size. Move the face slightly higher so the two stop competing. | Morrow | 3, 7 |
| 4 | Replace the plank panels with one big sweeping crescent mantle, two medium panels, and one small panel. Leave the back as a calm area. | Vesper | 2, 3, 4 |
| 5 | Double the thickness of the halo and fragments, and make the fragments chunkier. | Vesper | 1, 7 |
| 6 | Use the upper edge of the face patch as a brow that can tilt and pinch. Widen the mouth slightly. | Morrow | 6 |
| 7 | Add lid shapes that cut across the eyes for mischief, smugness, and embarrassment. Double the bead size. | Nix | 6, 7 |
| 8 | Lengthen the hands and fingers until one hand can arch over Morrow's head. | Vesper | 5 |
| 9 | Shorten the legs, broaden the feet, and taper the arms. Use two thick fingers and a thumb. | Morrow | 3, 5 |
| 10 | Replace the triangle fins with rounded paddle shapes that echo the crescent. | Nix | 2 |
| 11 | Design one signature rest pose and one hero pose per character, each with a clear line of action. | All | 8 |
| 12 | Design staging poses that let all three fit in a vertical frame. | All | 8, 9 |
| 13 | Give each signature feature a sound: an ember hum, a brass chime, a quick click. | All | presence |

**The top five upgrades fix most of the gap.** They move Vesper from the weakest
to a strong design, and they make all three focal features visible at phone size.

## Results after applying the upgrades (version 2)

All 13 upgrades were applied in `blender/stage2_blockout.py`, which now builds
version 2. Renders are in `blender/renders/stage2_v2`. Version 1 renders stay in
`blender/renders/stage2` for comparison.

### New scores

| Lens | Morrow | Vesper | Nix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Silhouette | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| 2. Shape language | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| 3. Big, medium, small | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| 4. Detail distribution | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| 5. Proportion and exaggeration | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| 6. Face and expression | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| 7. Focal feature | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| 8. Pose and line of action | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| 9. Contrast with the world | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| 10. Originality | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| **Total out of 50** | **39** (was 30) | **34** (was 21) | **37** (was 29) |

### What changed

- **Vesper no longer reads as No-Face.** The mask is now dark faceted stone with a
  crescent side profile, and the bright eyes are the first thing you see.
- **Every focal feature survives the thumbnail test.** Morrow's cavity and ember,
  Vesper's thicker halo, and Nix's bead and coral edge all read at small size.
- **Faces have range.** Morrow's brows can worry or set with determination. Nix's
  lid cuts give it a smug, innocent, or wide-eyed look.
- **Each character has a rest pose and a hero pose.** Morrow hugs a dream fragment
  at rest and braces with its crest high as a hero. Vesper stands formally at rest
  and bows into a deep crescent to shelter. Nix hovers smugly at rest and banks into
  a dart as a hero.
- **All three fit a 9:16 frame.** Vesper bows behind Morrow with one long hand over
  its head, and Nix perches on top of the halo.

### What is still weak

- **Vesper's front view still reads as a tall robed figure.** The big crescent
  mantle sweep shows from the side but is too subtle from the front.
- **The small hip panel on Vesper reads as a hanging tag.** It should be removed
  or merged into the crescent mantle.
- **Nix's sides are still flat slabs** when it turns edge-on.
- **Contrast with the world is untested.** It depends on stage 4 materials, the
  coral glow, and an actual environment.
- **Poses are hand-placed, not rigged.** None of the characters is animation-ready.

## Sound identity

A sound attached to each signature feature helps viewers recognize a character
even when it is off screen. These are design notes for whoever builds the sound.

| Character | Signature sound | When it plays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morrow | A soft, warm hum from the ember, like a held breath through a ceramic flute | Rises in pitch when curious, flutters when scared, steadies when brave |
| Vesper | Low brass chimes from the fragments, slightly out of tune with each other | Chimes slowly as the fragments drift. A sudden jangle when it loses composure |
| Nix | A quick, dry click and a short whoosh | The click when the bead snaps to a new target, the whoosh on every dart |

Keep each sound short and quiet enough to sit under dialogue and music. One sound
per character is enough. More becomes noise.

## Stage 3 refinement results (version 3)

Built by `blender/stage3_refinement.py`. Renders are in `blender/renders/stage3`.
Everything is still grayscale. No texture, fog, or glow was used to hide a shape.

### What was refined

| Area | Refinement |
| --- | --- |
| Morrow's shell | Gentle worn-stone surface variation, plus two large repaired seams. One runs into the cavity, one runs down the back. |
| Morrow's cavity | The rim is softened, so it reads as a carved opening rather than a boolean cut. |
| Morrow's face | Lower eyelids added. The face frame is fixed, so brows, lids, and mouth sit where they should. The mouth is now a light line that reads on the dark face. |
| Hands, all | Jointed tapering fingers with round tips, a cupped palm, and an opposable thumb. |
| Vesper's mantle | The big crescent panel now sweeps diagonally across the front. The stray hip panel is gone. Worn brass trim runs along that one leading edge only. |
| Vesper's arms | Flared sleeve shells over the upper arms echo the mantle panels. |
| Vesper's face | Lower lids added, and the upper lids now close in the right direction. |
| Vesper's fragments | Only the broken ends are jagged, so they read as halo pieces instead of rubble. |
| Nix's body | Rebuilt as a smooth pebble with a lens-shaped cross-section: thin edges, full belly. Edge-on, it is now a soft sliver instead of a slab. |
| Nix's eyes | The lids are cut directly into the eye shapes, so they never stick out past the body. |

### Expression range, tested

| Character | Expressions that now read without dialogue or glow |
| --- | --- |
| Morrow | Delight, worry, determination, embarrassment |
| Vesper | Concern, dry humour, fluster (uneven fragment spacing) |
| Nix | Mischief, innocence, embarrassment (turned edge-on) |

### A bug worth knowing about

Stage 2 built Morrow's face frame upside down. In the stage 2 renders, the arc above
Morrow's eyes was actually its mouth, and the bars below were its brows. Vesper's
upper lids also rotated backward into the head. Both are fixed in stage 3. The stage
2 script is left as it was, as a record.

### Still open

- **Vesper's front view** is improved by the diagonal crescent panel, but it still
  reads as a tall robed figure at thumbnail size.
- **Vesper's fragments** are blocky. They could be thinner and more elegant.
- **Nothing is rigged.** Every pose and expression is rebuilt from parameters, not
  posed with controls. Stage 5 turns these parameters into real rig controls and
  shape keys.
- **Contrast with the world** still waits on stage 4 materials and lighting.

## Stage 4 materials and lighting results (version 4)

Built by `blender/stage4_materials.py`. Renders are in `blender/renders/stage4`.

### Materials

| Character | Material contrast | Glow, and what it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morrow | Warm matte ivory shell with gentle colour variation and a little subsurface warmth, against a soft dark felt-like face and joints. Aged gold inlay in the repaired seams. Ember-toned ribbon crest. | The ember only. It is Morrow's feelings, and it lights the inside of the cavity. |
| Vesper | Matte midnight-blue mantle against a muted plum interior. Dark slate mask with subtle stone grain. Worn brass with uneven roughness on the halo, fragments, and one trim edge only. Muted plum hands, so gestures read against the mantle. | None. Vesper has no light of its own. Its brass only shines by reflecting others. |
| Nix | Near-black violet satin with a restrained cool sheen on the shadow side. Pale matte eyes. | The coral inner edge and the bead. The bead is its attention. |

### Lighting

- **Neutral studio first.** It uses a key, fill, and rim light on a plain floor, with
  exposure lowered so Morrow's ivory keeps its highlight detail.
- **Then one atmospheric dream-world shot.** It has an indigo-to-rose sky gradient,
  a dark plum ground, a distant broken crescent moon, and drifting dream shards. The
  lights are cool moonlight from behind, a low warm key, and a dim plum fill.
  There is no fog or bloom.

### Checks

- **Value hierarchy survives colour.** The studio lineup converted to luminance
  still reads light, dark, darkest, in that order.
- **Eyes stay readable without glow** in every expression, including Vesper's dark
  mask and Nix's near-black body.
- **Glow stays rare.** Three emitters in the whole cast, each with a meaning.

### Still open

- **Under the bright studio, Vesper's mantle reads deep blue** rather than
  blue-black. In the dream world it reads correctly. Exposure was lowered once, and
  could go further if wanted.
- **The panel interiors are not plum.** Only the inner core is. Giving each
  panel's inside face its own material needs a second material slot.
- **The dream world's far ground falls into a dark band** at the horizon. A curved
  backdrop or distant terrain would fix it.
- **Renders use Cycles.** The real-time Eevee renderer has not been tested.
- **Nothing is rigged yet.** That is stage 5.
