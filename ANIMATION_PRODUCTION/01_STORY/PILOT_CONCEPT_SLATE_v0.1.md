## Ground rules for this slate

- **Cast:** only the two approved references, the Girl and the Baker. The Inventor Boy and the Flower Grandmother aren't approved, so they don't appear. Working labels stand in until you approve names (recommended: Mila and Borek).
- **No dialogue.** Every concept is silent and carried by acting, Foley and music. That keeps them international, avoids casting voices before names are set, and puts the pilots' weight on the character acting the Bible defines (bunny grip, mustache tells, the bread hand-off).
- **Built for 9:16 from the start.** Each concept notes its vertical staging. Two-shots of the Girl and the Baker use depth (one near, one far) per the Character Lab's staging note. The Baker's beats are carried by brows and mustache because his eyes may not read at phone size.
- **World rules respected.** Physics are real, people get wet, the cat is a cat, and any strangeness stays ambiguous.
- **Lore flags.** Two concepts touch the village's unspoken past or its strangeness. They're marked **LORE** and need your sign-off on canon, not just on the story.
- **Sets.** Six of the nine concepts use the Bakery. One hero set built well serves most of the first season.

Beat timings are targets for the animatic, not locked edits.

## Tier 1: 15 seconds (quick experiments)

### P15-A · "Footprints" (RECOMMENDED)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | At dawn the Baker finds small sneaker prints in the flour on his floor. He follows them like a detective. They end at the counter, where the bunny sits alone. |
| **Emotion** | Curiosity, then delight |
| **Conflict** | Intruder versus territorial baker |
| **Motivations** | Baker: protect his kingdom. Girl: hungry, and too proud to ask. |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | Top-down shot of tiny sneaker prints crossing white flour. A huge clog lands beside them. |
| **Beats** | 0–2 s: hook. 2–6 s: Baker crouches low (comic for his build) and tracks the prints, mustache twitching. 6–10 s: prints stop at the counter. The bunny sits there. He leans in nose-to-nose with it, one brow up (Curious expression). 10–13 s: camera tilts **up** the tall frame to the top shelf, where the Girl is frozen with a roll in her mouth. 13–15 s: tilt back down. Where the roll was, she has left a wildflower in the basket. |
| **Visual payoff** | The vertical tilt-up reveal. It uses the 9:16 frame's height instead of fighting it. |
| **Ending** | The wildflower "payment". His mustache bristles up, which he can't control. |
| **Continuation** | Her payments become a running gag: a feather, a button, a snail shell. |
| **9:16 staging** | One continuous vertical axis: floor, counter, shelf. No two-shot problem. |
| **Complexity** | Low. One set, two characters, flour footprint decals and a dust puff. |

### P15-B · "Forecast"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | Rain. The Girl shelters under the bakery awning and drips land on the bunny. Inside, the Baker sees. |
| **Emotion** | Warmth through comedy |
| **Conflict** | His pride versus his concern |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | Extreme close-up: a fat raindrop hits the bunny's stitched face. |
| **Beats** | 0–2 s: hook. 2–6 s: the Girl pulls the bunny tighter (her fear tell) and the drips keep coming. 6–9 s: through the window, the Baker watches with his mustache drooping. 9–15 s: the drips stop. Cut wide: he stands outside holding a giant bread peel over her like an umbrella, staring at the sky as though he's only checking the weather. |
| **Visual payoff** | The bread peel as umbrella, and him refusing to look at her. |
| **Continuation** | Weather series: snow, heat and wind each force a grudging kindness. |
| **9:16 staging** | Her in the foreground at the bottom third; him tall in the depth behind her. |
| **Complexity** | Medium. Rain FX, wet materials, and both exterior and interior plates. |

### P15-C · "Customer"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | The Girl's coin is far too small. The Baker refuses, until he realises the customer is the bunny. |
| **Emotion** | Comedy |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | Low angle at her eye level. The Baker's crossed arms and glare fill the top of the frame. |
| **Beats** | 0–2 s: hook. 2–6 s: she slides a tiny coin across the counter. 6–10 s: his mustache bristles and he shakes his head. She raises the bunny to counter height and makes it "look" at him. 10–15 s: he holds out against the bunny, then breaks off the heel of a loaf and shoves it at the bunny without looking. The bunny "gives" it to her. |
| **Visual payoff** | His signature hand-off, aimed at a toy. |
| **Complexity** | Low. Single interior. |

## Tier 2: 25–30 seconds (core format)

### P30-A · "Mirror" (RECOMMENDED)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | The Girl ties the end of her braid under her nose as a mustache and copies the Baker's pose from across the square. He notices. It becomes a duel. |
| **Emotion** | Comedy, with warmth underneath |
| **Conflict** | Who wins the stand-off? Neither wants to be first to break. |
| **Motivations** | Girl: testing whether he'll play. Baker: dignity, which is failing. |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | The Girl straight to camera: braid mustache, arms crossed, ferocious scowl. |
| **Beats** | 0–2 s: hook. 2–7 s: reveal. Over the Baker's shoulder in the bakery doorway (foreground), she stands in the square (background), copying him. 7–12 s: he raises one brow and she raises one brow. He strokes his mustache and she strokes her braid. 12–19 s: escalation. He puffs his chest and she puffs hers and nearly topples. He does the full-bristle angry face and she does it too, with freckles bunching. 19–25 s: he plays his trump card, the bread hand-off. He shoves a loaf at her without looking. 25–30 s: still in character, she shoves the bunny back at him without looking. He's left holding it, baffled. His mustache lifts on its own, and he pushes it down with a finger. |
| **Visual payoff** | The role reversal: her bunny meets his bread. |
| **Ending** | His involuntary mustache smile, suppressed. |
| **Continuation** | Their first shared "game". Later episodes can call back the braid mustache. |
| **9:16 staging** | Over-the-shoulder depth shots alternate with mirrored singles (same lens, same framing) so the copying reads at a glance. A good early test of the depth staging the Character Lab recommends. |
| **Complexity** | Medium. Bakery doorway and the square. Strong acting test for both face rigs. |

### P30-B · "Knead"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | The Girl punches dough furiously in the empty bakery, and flour explodes everywhere. The Baker returns ready to roar, until he sees the bunny on the counter with one ear torn half off. |
| **Emotion** | Tenderness |
| **Conflict** | His anger at the mess versus understanding her grief |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | Small fists slam into dough and a flour cloud bursts toward the lens. |
| **Beats** | 0–2 s: hook. 2–7 s: he enters, arms crossed, full bristle. 7–11 s: he follows her look to the torn bunny. His mustache droops. 11–20 s: wordlessly, he stands behind her and guides her hands. The punching becomes his rhythmic kneading "dance". Her face moves from Angry through Worried to Curious. 20–27 s: time cut. He hands her the bunny, its ear sewn back on with baker's twine in the same criss-cross pattern as her braid cord. 27–30 s: she presses the bunny to her face. He's already turned away, busy. |
| **Visual payoff** | The twine stitch matching her braid. |
| **Continuation** | The repaired ear stays on the asset for the rest of the series as a visible history. Needs a Character Lab change request: a torn-ear variant and a twine-repair variant of the bunny. |
| **Complexity** | Medium. Flour particles and a bunny asset variant. |

### P30-C · "Burnt" (LORE)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | Smoke pours from the giant oven while the Baker stands frozen, staring at an old photograph propped by the oven. He has burnt a loaf, which never happens. |
| **Emotion** | Bittersweet; funny and sad at once |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | Black smoke rolls out of the oven mouth, and the Baker doesn't move. |
| **Beats** | 0–2 s: hook. 2–8 s: the Girl runs in and yanks the tray out. The loaf is black. 8–14 s: he reaches to throw it away. We glimpse the photograph but never see who is in it. 14–22 s: she breaks the loaf and eats the charcoal crust defiantly, grimacing and chewing. 22–30 s: his mustache slowly lifts. He breaks off a piece too, and both chew terrible bread, grimacing, neither willing to stop first. |
| **LORE flag** | Introduces the Baker's loss through an unseen photograph. Needs EP approval before it becomes canon. |
| **Complexity** | Medium. Smoke FX and a charred bread material. |

## Tier 3: 40–45 seconds (mini-stories)

### P45-A · "Display" (RECOMMENDED)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | Before dawn, the Girl sneaks into the bakery to bake the Baker a loaf, the first time she gives instead of takes. It comes out lopsided. She leaves it on his counter with the bunny guarding it, and hides. |
| **Emotion** | Heart-warming |
| **Conflict** | Her fear of being rejected versus his gruff standards |
| **Motivations** | Girl: to repay him the only way he understands. Baker: perfection, until this. |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | In the dark, small hands pull a tray from the glowing oven. On it sits a sad, lopsided loaf. |
| **Beats** | 0–2 s: hook. 2–10 s: she places the loaf, sets the bunny beside it as its guard, and squeezes it once (her uncertainty tell) before hiding behind flour sacks. 10–18 s: dawn. The Baker clomps in, stops, and studies the loaf with brows low and nothing readable. 18–26 s: he lifts it and turns it, then knocks on the bottom like an expert. The Girl clutches herself behind the sacks. We expect the bin. 26–36 s: he carries it to the shop window, removes his best loaf from the centre stand, and puts hers there in its place. 36–42 s: outside the window in the morning light, the Girl sees it, front and centre among perfect loaves. 42–45 s: inside, he sets the bunny on the counter facing the window, as though it's showing off too. |
| **Visual payoff** | The lopsided loaf in the place of honour. |
| **Ending** | No words. Neither acknowledges it, exactly as the Bible describes their relationship. |
| **Continuation** | The loaf stays in the window for days. It becomes a recurring background detail and a story of its own: "Does he ever sell it?" |
| **9:16 staging** | The window frame is naturally vertical. Shoot the window as a frame inside the frame from outside, with her reflection on the glass. |
| **Complexity** | Medium. Bakery set with night, dawn and morning lighting. This is a lighting showcase that reuses the Footprints set. |

### P45-B · "Downstream"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | The bunny drops from the Old Bridge into the stream. The Girl chases it out of the village. |
| **Emotion** | Tension, then relief |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | The bunny falls slowly from top to bottom of the tall frame toward the water. |
| **Beats** | 0–2 s: hook. 2–8 s: her horrified face, then she runs. Braid whips and sneakers slap. 8–20 s: she chases along the bank and slips. She's soaked (people get wet in this world). The bunny snags on a branch over the water. 20–30 s: she crawls out along the branch. It creaks and then cracks. 30–36 s: a huge hand grabs her collar from behind. It's the Baker, sleeves soaked; he saw her run past the bakery. He plucks the bunny out too. 36–45 s: the bakery. Both drip. The bunny dries on a bread peel by the oven among the loaves. |
| **Visual payoff** | The bunny drying among the bread. |
| **Complexity** | **High.** Water simulation, bridge and stream sets, wet hair and cloth. Not suited to an early pilot. |

### P45-C · "The Little Door" (LORE)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | A small round door has appeared in the bakery wall beside the oven. It wasn't there yesterday. |
| **Emotion** | Mystery |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | The Girl knocks on a door no bigger than her bunny. |
| **Beats** | 0–2 s: hook. 2–10 s: she tries the latch. It's locked. 10–20 s: she tugs the Baker over. He sees it and freezes. This isn't surprise; he recognises it. 20–30 s: he shoves flour sacks in front of it and distracts her with bread, too quickly. 30–40 s: night, the empty bakery. 40–45 s: warm light leaks from under the little door, and a single wildflower slides out across the floor. |
| **LORE flag** | Establishes ambiguous magic inside the bakery and a secret the Baker keeps. It's a strong series hook, but it needs EP approval on canon. |
| **Complexity** | Medium. |

## Recommendation: three pilots

| Tier | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 15 s | **P15-A "Footprints"** | Clearest hook on the slate. Uses vertical height as the reveal. Lowest risk. |
| 25–30 s | **P30-A "Mirror"** | Best test of both characters' signature gestures and the depth-staged 9:16 two-shot. Pure comedy. |
| 40–45 s | **P45-A "Display"** | The emotional spine of the series (Bible section 7) in one silent story. Lighting showcase. |

**Why these three together**
- **Three tones:** mystery-comedy, comedy, emotional. The analytics will tell us which register the audience wants from these characters.
- **One hero set:** all three need only the Bakery, plus the square seen from its doorway. That matters while there's no Blender in either department's environment.
- **No lore commitments.** None of the three needs a canon decision from you.
- **A shared prop language** (wildflower, bunny, bread) seeds recurring motifs with no extra assets.

**Release order:** Footprints first, because the strongest hook gives the cleanest first data point. Then Mirror, then Display.

## Dependencies and blockers

1. **Blender.** It isn't installed here, and per `STATUS.md` it isn't in the Character Lab either. Final animation is blocked until it's available. Story, storyboards and animatics can proceed now.
2. **Character assets.** The Girl, the Baker and the bunny prop are needed. "Knead" would also need bunny damage and repair variants (change request to the Character Lab if it's chosen).
3. **Props for the recommended set:** loaves (perfect and lopsided), bread peel, flour sacks, shelves, a wildflower, a small coin, the oven and the shop window. These go in the environment and prop plan at the pre-production stage.
4. **EP decisions needed:** approve or redirect the three picks; rule on the LORE items (photograph, little door); confirm names before any on-screen text, titles or captions use them.
