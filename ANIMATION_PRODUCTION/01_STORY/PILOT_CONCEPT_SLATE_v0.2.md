## Ground rules for this slate

- **Cast:** Mila and Borek only. Pim and Vela have approved names, but their designs aren't approved yet, so they don't appear in pilots.
- **Silent-first.** Characters vocalise but speak no words. Shop signs are pictures, so no on-screen text appears inside the story.
- **Built for 9:16 from the start.** Two-shots stage Mila near the camera and Borek in the mid-ground, matching the Character Lab's 9:16 two-shot schematic. Borek's beats are carried by his brows and mustache because his eyes may not read at phone size.
- **Tempo contrast is a comedy tool.** Mila resolves actions in 6–10 frames with short holds. Borek takes 8–12-frame anticipations and holds for 1–2 seconds, and his mustache trails his head by 3–6 frames.
- **One central mystery.** The crossed stitch on the bunny matches the cord binding Mila's braid. Pilots may show the stitch but never explain it. The answer is the EP's.
- **No magic.** The world is grounded. Any strangeness is a visual clue, not the supernatural.
- **One hero set.** The bakery interior uses smoke-darkened plaster and dark wood. Every recommended pilot uses it.

Beat timings are targets for the animatic, not locked edits.

## Tier 1: 15 seconds (quick experiments)

### P15-A · "Footprints" (RECOMMENDED)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | At dawn Borek finds small sneaker prints in the flour on his floor. He follows them like a detective. They end at the counter, where the bunny sits alone. |
| **Emotion** | Curiosity, then delight |
| **Conflict** | An intruder versus a territorial baker |
| **Motivations** | Borek: protect his kingdom. Mila: hungry, and too proud to ask. |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | Top-down shot of tiny sneaker prints crossing white flour. A heavy clog *clop* is heard before the clog lands beside them (Borek's sound hook). |
| **Beats** | **0–2 s:** hook. **2–6 s:** Borek crouches low, slowly (comic for his build), and tracks the prints. His mustache trails each head turn. **6–10 s:** the prints stop at the counter. The bunny sits there, its crossed stitch catching the light. Borek leans in nose-to-nose with it, one brow raised. A held stare. **10–13 s:** the camera tilts **up** the tall frame to the top shelf. Mila is frozen with a roll in her mouth; frozen stillness is her "something is wrong" tell. **13–15 s:** tilt back down. Where the roll was, she has left a wildflower in the basket. |
| **Visual payoff** | The vertical tilt-up reveal uses the height of the 9:16 frame instead of fighting it. |
| **Ending** | The wildflower "payment". Borek's mustache bristles upward, which he can't control. |
| **Continuation** | Her payments become a running gag: a feather, a button, a snail shell. |
| **9:16 staging** | One continuous vertical axis: floor, counter, shelf. No side-by-side two-shot. Mila on the shelf is framed against dark wood and plaster, not brick. |
| **Sound** | Clog clops, floury footstep scuffs, one sniff from Borek, a tiny chewing sound from above before the tilt. |
| **Complexity** | Low. One set, two characters, flour footprint decals and a dust puff. |

### P15-B · "Forecast"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | Rain. Mila shelters under the bakery awning and drips land on the bunny. Inside, Borek sees. |
| **Emotion** | Warmth through comedy |
| **Conflict** | His pride versus his concern |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | Extreme close-up: a fat raindrop hits the bunny's stitched face. |
| **Beats** | **0–2 s:** hook. **2–6 s:** Mila pulls the bunny tighter (her fear tell) and the drips keep coming. **6–9 s:** through the window, Borek watches, mustache drooping. **9–15 s:** the drips stop. Cut wide: Borek stands outside holding a giant bread peel over her like an umbrella, staring at the sky as though he's only checking the weather. |
| **Visual payoff** | The bread peel as umbrella, and him refusing to look at her. |
| **Continuation** | Weather series: snow, heat and wind each force a grudging kindness. |
| **9:16 staging** | Mila in the foreground at the bottom third; Borek tall in the depth behind her. |
| **Complexity** | Medium. Rain FX, wet materials, and both exterior and interior plates. |

### P15-C · "Customer"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | Mila's coin is far too small. Borek refuses, until he realises the customer is the bunny. |
| **Emotion** | Comedy |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | Low angle at her eye level. Borek's crossed arms and glare fill the top of the frame. |
| **Beats** | **0–2 s:** hook. **2–6 s:** she slides a tiny coin across the counter. **6–10 s:** his mustache bristles and he shakes his head, slowly. She raises the bunny to counter height and makes it "look" at him. **10–15 s:** he holds out against the bunny, then breaks off the heel of a loaf and shoves it at the bunny without looking. The bunny "gives" it to her. |
| **Visual payoff** | His signature bread shove, aimed at a toy. |
| **Complexity** | Low. Single interior. |

## Tier 2: 25–30 seconds (core format)

### P30-A · "Mirror" (RECOMMENDED)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | Mila holds the end of her braid under her nose as a mustache and copies Borek's pose from across the square. He notices. It becomes a duel. |
| **Emotion** | Comedy, with warmth underneath |
| **Conflict** | Who breaks first? Neither will. |
| **Motivations** | Mila: testing whether he'll play. Borek: his dignity, which is failing. |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | Mila straight to camera: braid mustache, arms crossed, ferocious scowl. |
| **Beats** | **0–2 s:** hook. **2–7 s:** reveal. Over Borek's shoulder in the bakery doorway (foreground), Mila stands in the square (background), copying him. **7–12 s:** he raises one brow (slow), and she raises one brow (instant). He strokes his mustache, and she strokes her braid. **12–19 s:** escalation. He puffs his chest; she puffs hers and nearly topples. He gives the full bristle; she copies it, freckles bunching. **19–25 s:** his trump card, the bread shove. He thrusts a loaf at her without looking. **25–30 s:** still in character, she shoves the bunny at him without looking. He's left holding it, baffled. His mustache lifts on its own and he presses it down with one finger. |
| **Visual payoff** | The role reversal: her bunny meets his bread. |
| **Ending** | His involuntary mustache smile, suppressed. |
| **Comedy engine** | Tempo contrast. Every move he makes in 1–2 seconds, she copies in a fraction of a second. The joke lands through timing, which suits silent acting at phone size. |
| **Continuation** | Their first shared "game". Later episodes can call back the braid mustache. |
| **9:16 staging** | Over-the-shoulder depth shots alternate with mirrored singles (same lens, same framing) so the copying reads at a glance. Mila is backed by stone or sky in the square, not terracotta roofs. A strong early test of the depth staging the Character Lab recommends. |
| **Sound** | Matched vocal grunts, the creak of his jacket buttons as he puffs, her sneaker squeak as she wobbles. |
| **Complexity** | Medium. Bakery doorway plus the square. Strong acting test for both face rigs. |

## Tier 3: 40–45 seconds (mini-stories)

### P45-A · "Display" (RECOMMENDED)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | Before dawn, Mila sneaks into the bakery to bake Borek a loaf, the first time she gives instead of takes. It comes out lopsided. She leaves it on his counter with the bunny guarding it, and hides. |
| **Emotion** | Heart-warming |
| **Conflict** | Her fear of being rejected versus his gruff standards |
| **Motivations** | Mila: repay him in the only language he understands. Borek: perfection, until now. |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | In the dark, small hands pull a tray from the glowing wood-fired oven. On it sits a sad, lopsided loaf. |
| **Beats** | **0–2 s:** hook. **2–10 s:** she places the loaf, sets the bunny beside it as its guard, and squeezes it once (her uncertainty tell) before hiding behind the flour sacks. **10–18 s:** dawn. Clogs are heard first. Borek enters, stops, and studies the loaf with brows low. A long, unreadable hold. **18–26 s:** he lifts it, turns it, and knocks on the bottom like an expert. Behind the sacks, Mila clutches herself. We expect the bin. **26–36 s:** he carries it to the shop window, removes his best loaf from the centre stand, and puts hers in its place. **36–42 s:** outside in the morning light, Mila sees it through the glass, front and centre among perfect loaves. **42–45 s:** inside, Borek sets the bunny on the counter facing the window, as though it's showing off too. |
| **Visual payoff** | The lopsided loaf in the place of honour. |
| **Ending** | No words. Neither acknowledges it. |
| **Optional clue (EP call)** | In the 10–18 s beat, Borek ties on his oldest apron. Its patch shows the crossed stitch, visible for under a second and unremarked. This is a sanctioned clue site in Bible v0.5. Including it in pilot 3 starts the mystery early; leaving it out saves it for later. |
| **Continuation** | The loaf stays in the window for days as a recurring background detail. Does he ever sell it? |
| **9:16 staging** | The shop window is naturally vertical. Shoot it as a frame inside the frame from outside, with Mila's reflection on the glass. |
| **Lighting** | Night (oven glow only), then dawn (cool window light), then morning (warm exterior). A lighting showcase on the same set as Footprints. |
| **Complexity** | Medium. Bakery set with three lighting states. |

### P45-B · "Downstream"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Premise** | The bunny drops from the Old Bridge into the stream. Mila chases it out of the village. |
| **Emotion** | Tension, then relief |
| **Hook (0–2 s)** | The bunny falls slowly from top to bottom of the tall frame toward the water. |
| **Beats** | **0–2 s:** hook. **2–8 s:** her horrified face, then she runs: braid whipping, sneakers slapping. **8–20 s:** she chases along the bank, slips and is soaked. The bunny snags on a branch over the water. **20–30 s:** she crawls out along the branch. It creaks and then cracks. **30–36 s:** a huge hand grabs her collar from behind. It's Borek, sleeves soaked; he saw her run past the bakery. He plucks the bunny out too. **36–45 s:** back at the bakery, both dripping. The bunny dries on a bread peel by the oven among the loaves. |
| **Visual payoff** | The bunny drying among the bread. |
| **Complexity** | **High.** Water simulation, bridge and stream sets, wet hair and cloth. Not suited to an early pilot, especially with CPU-only rendering. |

## Recommendation: three pilots

| Tier | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 15 s | **P15-A "Footprints"** | Clearest hook on the slate, with a sound hook built in. Uses vertical height as the reveal. Lowest risk. |
| 25–30 s | **P30-A "Mirror"** | Best test of both characters' signature gestures, the tempo contrast and the depth-staged 9:16 two-shot. Pure comedy. |
| 40–45 s | **P45-A "Display"** | The Mila–Borek emotional core in one silent story. Lighting showcase. |

**Why these three together**
- **Three tones:** mystery-comedy, comedy, emotional. Analytics will show which register the audience wants from these characters.
- **One hero set:** all three use the bakery interior, plus the square seen from its doorway. That matters with CPU-only rendering on four threads.
- **No new canon.** The only open lore choice is the optional apron clue in Display.
- **Shared props** (wildflower, bunny, bread) seed recurring motifs with no extra assets.

**Release order:** Footprints, then Mirror, then Display. The strongest hook gives the cleanest first data point.
